Risks and Rewards


Summarize the risks of the proposed project and how they will be addressed.



Risks of the Proposed Agriculture Project and How They Will Be Addressed



The proposed agriculture project may face several risks, including climate and weather risks, pests and diseases, water shortages, market-price fluctuations, rising input costs, and limited technical
knowledge. Extreme weather such as floods, droughts, or storms could reduce crop yields. Pests and diseases may also damage crops and livestock.



These risks will be addressed through climate-resilient farming practices, proper irrigation and drainage, use of improved and disease-resistant seeds, integrated pest management, regular monitoring, and access to agricultural
extension services. The project will also diversify crops and production activities to reduce dependence on a single source of income .



Market risks will be managed by identifying buyers in advance, improving storage and post-harvest handling, and developing links with local markets and cooperatives. Financial risks will be reduced
through careful budgeting, phased investment, and maintaining an emergency reserve.



Overall, the project will use regular monitoring, risk assessments, farmer training, and contingency planning to identify problems early and take corrective action. These measures will help improve the
project's sustainability and reduce potential losses.

Key Issues


Identify key decisions and issues that need immediate or near-term resolution.
Immediate decisions — next few days :



Project scope and priorities : 1.Confirm target crops, geographic coverage, beneficiaries, and expected outputs. 2. Resolve any competing priorities between production, livelihoods, food security, or commercial objectives.
Land and site readiness : 1.Confirm land availability, ownership/use rights, soil condition, irrigation access, and suitability for the selected crops. 2. Identify sites that may need rehabilitation or alternative locations.
Crop and production plan: 1.Finalize crop varieties, planting calendar, acreage, input requirements, and expected yields. 2.Ensure decisions account for local climate and seasonal risks.
Inputs and procurement: 1.Confirm availability and procurement of seeds, fertilizer, pesticides/biological controls, equipment, and other inputs. 2. Resolve procurement lead times and budget gaps before the planting window.
Water and irrigation: 1.Confirm water availability, irrigation infrastructure, operating costs, and responsibilities for maintenance. 2. Address any immediate water-supply constraints.


Near-term decisions — next 2–8 weeks

Budget and financing : 1. Confirm the project budget, funding availability, cash-flow requirements, and contingency reserve. 2. Resolve major cost overruns or funding gaps.
Implementation responsibilities : 1.Clearly assign responsibilities among project management, farmers/cooperatives, extension staff, contractors, and government/local partners. 2. Establish
decision-making and escalation mechanisms.

Market and commercialization : 1.Confirm buyers, market access, expected prices, storage, transportation, and aggregation arrangements. 2.Avoid expanding production without a
credible route to market.
Farmer participation and capacity : 1. Finalize beneficiary selection and participation arrangements. 2. Identify training, extension, and technical-support requirements.
Risk management : 1. Address exposure to drought/flooding, pests and diseases, input-price volatility, market-price changes, labor shortages, and extreme weather. 2. Establish contingency
measures and trigger points.
Monitoring and results : 1.Agree on key indicators such as area cultivated, survival/yield rates, farmer participation, production costs, income, and project milestones. 2.Establish who collects
and reviews the data and how often.
A useful management approach is to classify each issue as: Decision required → Owner → Deadline → Consequence if delayed → Recommended action. This makes the project's immediate priorities much
easier to track.

State consequences of decision postponement : ighest-risk decisions to resolve first : 1. Planting schedule → 2. Inputs/procurement → 3. Water/irrigation → 4. Financing → 5. Land readiness → 6. Market arrangements.
